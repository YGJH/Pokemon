\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document describes the MCTS system \emph{as it currently exists in the
code}, not as designed. It covers the observation encoding, the action space,
the PUCT search, the self-play training loop, and the inference path used by
the Kaggle submission. Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the measured problem:
with a repaired critic and a corrected value backup, PUCT still loses
$40$ percentage points against its own greedy policy prior. Every number here
was measured on this repository; every file:line reference was read, not
recalled.
\end{abstract}

\section{System overview}

Three components cooperate at inference time:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Policy network} (\file{ptcg\_il/model/policy.py}) --- a
    transformer over $46$ state tokens producing per-option logits (the
    \emph{pointer head}), a scalar value, and four \emph{belief} heads over
    opponent cards.
  \item \textbf{Belief $\to$ deck oracle}
    (\file{ptcg\_il/search\_infer.py:90}) --- converts the belief heads into a
    concrete $60$-card opponent decklist.
  \item \textbf{PUCT tree} (\file{ptcg\_search/src/puct.rs}, Rust, driven over
    a C-ABI) --- searches with the policy as prior and the value head as leaf
    evaluation, determinized against the predicted opponent deck.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The division of labour matters and is a common source of confusion:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Head} & \textbf{Predicts} & \textbf{Consumed by} \\
\midrule
Pointer & distribution over \emph{legal options} & PUCT priors; greedy action \\
Value   & game outcome $\in(-1,1)$              & PUCT leaf backup \\
Belief (\code{arch}, \code{deck}, & distributions over \emph{opponent cards} & determinization \\
\quad \code{hidden}, \code{hand}) & & (never over actions) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\textbf{The belief heads never score a move.} They exist solely to supply a
concrete opponent deck so that a hidden-information game can be rolled out.

\label{sec:obs}

Produced by \code{featurize()} in \file{ptcg\_il/featurizer.py}. Pure NumPy,
one call per decision point.

\subsection{State token sequence}

A fixed sequence of $L_{\text{STATE}} = 46$ tokens:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Rows} & \textbf{Count} & \textbf{Token} & \textbf{Layout} \\
\midrule

$13$--$42$ & $30$ & HAND     & my hand, packed to front \\

\noindent
Dense per-token features: \code{poke\_feat} $[12,26]$, \code{hand\_feat}
$[30,2]$, \code{sum\_feat} $[2,11]$, \code{cls\_feat} $[93]$,
\code{opt\_scalar} $[64,6]$. Masks: \code{tok\_mask} $[46]$,
\code{opt\_mask} $[64]$, \code{discard\_mask} $[2,60]$.

\subsection{Card features: $94$ dimensions}

\begin{equation*}
\underbrace{\text{base}[52]}_{\substack{\text{hp}/400,\ \text{retreat}/4,\ \text{cardType}(7),\ \text{stage}(3),\\ \text{energyType}(12),\ \text{weakness}(12),\ \text{resistance}(12),\\ [\text{ex},\text{megaEx},\text{tera},\text{aceSpec}](4)}}
\;\Vert\;
\underbrace{3 \times \text{attack}[14]}_{\substack{\text{damage}/350,\ \text{energy-cost histogram}(12),\\ |\text{energies}|/5}}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
Built by \code{card\_static\_row()} in \file{ptcg\_mine/cards.py}. Because
\code{card.attacks} holds attack \emph{ids}, the builder requires an
\code{attacks\_by\_id} lookup; without it the $52$--$94$ half is silently zero.
Measured on this corpus: $1055$ of $1267$ engine cards have a non-empty attack
half.

\subsection{Card identity uses raw engine ids}

Features are looked up by \textbf{engine card id}, never by vocab index:

\begin{lstlisting}
_cfeat = lambda ids: _ids_to_feat(ids, engine_card_features, F_CARD, None)
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
This matters for generalization. \code{engine\_card\_features.npy} covers all
$1267$ engine cards; the mined vocab holds $294$. Routing through the vocab
mapped the other $973$ to \code{UNKNOWN\_CARD}, which dereferences to the
string \code{"UNKNOWN"} and therefore to an \emph{all-zero row} ---
indistinguishable from an empty slot. That never fires on the training corpus
(the vocab is built \code{all\_corpus}) and only appears in live play.
The same applied to attacks: $1341$ of $1556$ were outside the attack vocab.
\code{PAD} remains $0$ and no engine id is $0$, so mask logic is unaffected.

\section{Action space}
\label{sec:action}

\subsection{Options}

Each decision offers up to $O_{\text{MAX}} = 64$ options. An action is a list
of \emph{distinct option indices} $k$ with
$\text{minCount} \le k \le \text{maxCount}$.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Single-select} ($\text{maxCount} = 1$): one index.
  \item \textbf{Multi-select} ($\text{maxCount} > 1$): handled
    autoregressively, pick-without-replacement, with an appended \textbf{STOP}
    column (\code{opt\_type} $= 17$) at index \code{stop\_column}. STOP is
    masked until $t \ge \text{minCount}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pointer head}

\code{PointerHead.forward(h, tok\_mask, card\_enc, x)} returns
\code{(logits, o)}. Each option token is built from its type embedding, the
gathered encoder states at \code{opt\_src\_idx} / \code{opt\_tgt\_idx}, its
card and attack features, and \code{opt\_scalar}; it then cross-attends over
the $46$ state tokens. Padding is masked to $-10^9$.

\section{PUCT search}
\label{sec:search}

Implemented in Rust (\file{ptcg\_search/src/puct.rs}), driven from Python over
a C-ABI (\file{ptcg\_il/search\_infer.py}).

\subsection{Protocol}

\begin{lstlisting}
handle = puct_init(obs_json, libcg_path, fixed_deck, opp_deck,
                   iterations, c_puct, seed, host_initialized=1)
loop:
    leaf = puct_select(handle)      # -> leaf_obs_json, player_role,
                                    #    n_options, is_terminal, tree_done
    if leaf.error or leaf.tree_done: break
    priors, value = _policy_evaluate_leaf(leaf.leaf_obs_json, ...)
    puct_expand(handle, priors, value)
result = puct_result(handle)        # -> visit_counts, root_value
puct_free(handle)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Selection}

For a child $c$ of a node with visit count $N$ and prior $P$:
\begin{equation*}
Q(c) = \frac{\text{total\_value}(c)}{N(c)}, \qquad
U(c) = c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(c) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{N}}{1 + N(c)}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\text{score}(c) =
\begin{cases}
Q(c) + U(c) & \text{\code{player\_role}} = 0 \quad (\text{our turn, max}) \\
Q(c) - U(c) & \text{\code{player\_role}} = 1 \quad (\text{opponent, min})
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Value sign convention --- critical}
\label{sec:sign}

\code{puct.rs:53} states that \code{total\_value} is a
\emph{``Sum of backpropagated values (from our perspective, in $[-1,1]$)''},
and \code{backpropagate} adds the value \textbf{unchanged} along the selection
path. The min/max distinction is expressed only through the sign of $U$ above.

The network does \emph{not} share this convention. In
\file{ptcg\_il/shard\_writer.py:454}:
\begin{lstlisting}
value_target = 1.0 if r == 1 else -1.0   # r = reward of the player
                                         #     whose observation this is
\end{lstlisting}
so the value head predicts from the \textbf{acting player's} perspective.
Therefore at an opponent leaf the prediction must be negated:
\begin{equation*}
v_{\text{backup}} =
\begin{cases}
+v_\theta(s_{\text{leaf}}) & \text{\code{player\_role}} = 0 \\
-v_\theta(s_{\text{leaf}}) & \text{\code{player\_role}} = 1
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Priors need no such flip: they score the options belonging to whoever moves at
that leaf, which is exactly that node's action set.

\subsection{Determinization}

MCTS cannot roll out hidden information, so \code{puct\_init} is given a
concrete \code{opp\_deck}. It comes from \code{predict\_opponent\_deck()}
(\file{search\_infer.py:90}), a four-tier fallback:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Learned \code{arch} head} --- if $\max_i \text{softmax}(\text{arch})_i \ge 0.5$
    and that archetype has a $60$-card representative, use it.
  \item \textbf{\code{ArchetypePosterior}} --- Bayesian elimination over
    archetypes using opponent cards actually observed so far.
  \item \textbf{Card distribution head} --- bag-of-cards sampling from the
    \code{deck} logits.
  \item \textbf{Empty list} --- Rust falls back to mirroring our own deck.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
This is a \textbf{point estimate}: one deck per decision, and all
$K$ determinizations and all iterations assume it.

\section{Self-play training}
\label{sec:training}

\file{ptcg\_rl/mcts\_train.py}. One iteration:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Self-play} --- \code{games\_per\_iter} games, our fixed deck
    versus an opponent archetype sampled from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{opp}}$
    (or all $179$ with \code{--all-archetypes}). Opponents are sampled with
    adaptive weights: low win rate $\to$ high weight.
  \item \textbf{MCTS tagging} --- a fraction $\rho$ of decisions
    (\code{--rho}) get PUCT targets computed with the \emph{known} opponent
    deck, aggregated over $K$ determinizations:
    \begin{equation*}
      \tilde{\pi}(a) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} N_k(a)}{\sum_{a'}\sum_{k=1}^{K} N_k(a')},
      \qquad
      \tilde{V} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} V_k^{\text{root}}
    \end{equation*}
  \item \textbf{Replay buffer} --- stores \code{features}, \code{action\_idx},
    \code{action\_len}, \code{mcts\_pi}, \code{mcts\_value},
    \code{game\_outcome}, \code{opp\_archetype}. Capacity
    \code{--buffer-capacity}; training starts at \code{--min-buffer}.
  \item \textbf{Optimisation} --- \code{train\_steps\_per\_iter} steps.
  \item \textbf{League evaluation} --- every \code{--eval-every-games}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Loss}

Per \code{train\_step()} (\file{mcts\_train.py:889}):
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L} = c_\pi \mathcal{L}_\pi + c_V \mathcal{L}_V + \beta\, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}
\end{equation*}
with, for each sample,
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_\pi =
\begin{cases}
-\sum_{a} \tilde{\pi}(a) \log p_\theta(a \mid s) & \text{if MCTS-tagged (distillation)} \\[0.4em]
-\log p_\theta(\mathbf{a}^{\text{expert}} \mid s) & \text{otherwise (behaviour cloning,} \\[-0.2em]
 & \text{\quad joint AR log-prob incl.\ STOP)}
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_V = \big(v_\theta(s) - z\big)^2, \quad z \in \{-1,+1\} \text{ the game outcome}
\end{equation*}
and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}$ an anchor to the frozen $\pi_{\text{IL}}$.

\subsection{League gate}

A candidate is saved \textbf{only} if its Elo reaches the $70$th percentile of
the pool (\code{elo.top\_pct(0.70)}, logged as \code{top30\%}); at most
\code{--max-champions} champions are retained. Past champions and all
discovered IL checkpoints are registered as opponents, with \code{ckpt-best}
fixed at Elo $1500$ as the anchor.

\section{Inference procedure}
\label{sec:inference}

\code{agent(obs\_dict)} in the generated \file{main.py}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{obs.select is None} $\Rightarrow$ return the fixed $60$-card deck
    (deck-selection step) and reset the observed-card tracker.
  \item \code{extract\_opp\_visible\_cards()} --- accumulate opponent cards
    seen so far, for the Bayesian tier of the deck oracle.
  \item \code{predict\_opponent\_deck()} --- belief heads $\to$ $60$-card list.
  \item \code{mcts\_search()} $\to$ \code{result["indices"]}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Action extraction}

\code{puct\_result} returns \code{visit\_counts} as
$[(\text{option\_index}, \text{visits}), \dots]$ and has \textbf{no}
\code{indices} key. The move is the most-visited root child:
\begin{equation*}
a^\star = \arg\max_{a} N(a)
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Multi-select is not searched}

The tree applies \emph{one} option index per node
(\code{search\_step(id, \&[action])}), so root visit counts rank the
\emph{first} pick only. That is a complete action for single-select but not a
$k$-subset for multi-select. Decisions with $\text{maxCount} > 1$ therefore
bypass the tree entirely and use the policy's autoregressive
\code{select\_multi}. Taking the tree's top-$k$ would report a choice the
search never evaluated, and might not even be a legal co-selection.

\subsection{Graceful degradation}

Any failure inside \code{mcts\_search} falls back to \code{\_greedy\_action}
(a single forward pass). This fallback is now \textbf{logged once per distinct
reason}. Previously it was a bare \code{except Exception: pass}, under which a
run with no search at all was indistinguishable from a working one --- the
agent still returned legal moves, just from the policy prior.

\section{Current problem}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Measured results}

Head-to-head against \file{700.py}, archetype-0 mirror match, identical
weights (\file{checkpoints\_a0\_r1/ckpt-rl-last.pt}), search toggled:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Games} & \textbf{Win rate} & \textbf{95\% CI} & \textbf{s/game} \\
\midrule
Greedy (no search)                & $60$ & $\mathbf{76.7\%}$ & $[64.6, 85.6]$ & $0.3$ \\
PUCT, broken critic, sign bug     & $60$ & $41.7\%$ & --- & $4.0$ \\
PUCT, repaired critic, sign bug   & $60$ & $25.0\%$ & --- & $3.7$ \\
PUCT, repaired critic, sign fixed & $60$ & $36.7\%$ & $[25.6, 49.3]$ & $4.9$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Greedy versus corrected PUCT: $\Delta = -40.0$ points, $z = 4.42$,
non-overlapping confidence intervals. Search costs $16\times$ the wall clock
to lose $40$ points.

\subsection{Critic status}

R1 critic repair (\file{ptcg\_rl/critic.py}, Phase A: trunk frozen, $4$
value-head tensors) passed its gate:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Before} & \textbf{After R1} & \textbf{Gate} \\
\midrule
\code{corr}            & weak     & $0.4837$ & $\ge 0.35$ \checkmark \\
\code{pred\_std}       & $0.068$  & $0.4246$ & $\ge 0.30$ \checkmark \\
\code{sign\_agreement} & $0.6476$ & $0.6910$ & --- \\
per-turn accuracy      & ---      & $0.61 \to 0.70 \to 0.75 \to 0.74$ & rises \checkmark \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Phase B (joint fine-tune) was never needed, so $h_{\text{CLS}}$ did carry
outcome signal that IL's auxiliary MSE had simply not taught the head to read.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The critic passing its gate did \emph{not} make search competitive, so
``undertrained critic'' is not a sufficient explanation. But the critic is
still weak in absolute terms: \code{sign\_agreement} $0.69$ means roughly one
backup in three has the wrong sign, and early-game per-turn accuracy $0.61$ is
barely above chance. PUCT backs up $64$ such evaluations per decision.

\noindent
The two available search measurements differ in \emph{two} variables at once
(critic quality and the sign bug), so neither isolates the critic's
contribution.

\subsection{Open hypotheses}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Critic still too weak.} Discriminator: rerun with
    $v \equiv 0$ (priors and visit counts only). Recovery toward $41\%$
    implicates the critic; staying near $37\%$ exonerates it.
  \item \textbf{Determinization quality.} The opponent deck is a point
    estimate from an \code{arch} head that only commits at $p \ge 0.5$. If it
    is wrong, all $64$ iterations plan against a fiction. Discriminator:
    measure predicted-vs-true archetype agreement in self-play, where the true
    deck is known.
  \item \textbf{Iteration budget.} $64$ iterations over a branching factor of
    $2$--$10$. Ranked last: more iterations of a mis-specified search only
    compound the error.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope caveat}

All numbers above are a single-deck (archetype $0$) mirror match against one
opponent. Per the project's own record, offline and live rankings have
diverged before ($78\%$ vs $48\%$), so these should not be generalized to the
competition ladder without a wider test.

\section{Defect history in this path}

Six defects sat between \code{mcts\_search} and a working tree. All were
masked by the blanket \code{except Exception: pass}, which is why the loud
fallback warning had to be added \emph{first} --- each fix merely revealed the
next.

\begin{center}
\begin{longtable}{@{}rp{6.6cm}p{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{Defect} & \textbf{Effect} \\
\midrule
\endhead
$1$ & \code{\_find\_libcg()} returned a bare \code{"libcg.so"}; its
      \code{from cg.sim import \_lib\_path} fallback used a name that does not
      exist (\code{sim.py} has a \emph{local} \code{lib\_path})
    & \code{puct\_init} $\to$ NULL; every decision greedy \\
\addlinespace
$2$ & Read \code{leaf["obs\_json"]}; Rust emits \code{leaf\_obs\_json}
    & \code{KeyError} on iteration $1$ \\
\addlinespace
$3$ & \code{policy.\_encode()} returns \code{(h, history\_h)};
      \code{policy.pointer(h, opt\_mask)} predates the signature
      \code{(h, tok\_mask, card\_enc, x)}; \code{value[0,0]} on a $[B]$ tensor
    & \code{TypeError} on every leaf \\
\addlinespace
$4$ & \code{puct\_result} returns \code{visit\_counts}, no \code{indices} key,
      but \code{main.py} did \code{result.get("indices", [])}
    & successful search returned an \emph{empty} action; engine
      \code{IndexError} \\
\addlinespace
$5$ & \code{player\_role} ignored; value not negated at opponent nodes
      (\S\ref{sec:sign})
    & inverted backup on half the leaves; harmless while the critic was
      near-constant, harmful once R1 gave it discriminative power \\
\addlinespace
$6$ & \code{\_dict\_to\_batch} filtered on \code{isinstance(v, np.ndarray)},
      but the featurizer emits scalars as \code{np.int64} (an
      \code{np.generic})
    & $9$ keys silently dropped, incl.\ \code{minCount};
      \code{KeyError: 'minCount'} on multi-select only \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
\end{center}

\noindent
Defect $6$ has a subtlety worth recording: the naive repair
(\code{np.ascontiguousarray}) promotes a $0$-d scalar to $1$-d, yielding shape
$[1,1]$ instead of the documented $[B]$. That broadcasts wrongly inside
\code{\_select\_multi\_raw} \emph{without raising}, so the \code{KeyError}
would have disappeared while STOP masking quietly misbehaved.
